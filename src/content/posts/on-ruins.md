A ruin is a building that has stopped being useful and started being beautiful. The roof is gone, the windows are empty, and suddenly we can see the sky through it, which is perhaps the only thing it was ever really for.

The eighteenth century built fake ruins on purpose, in the gardens of country houses, so that the owners could walk past them and feel the right things. We laugh at that now. But most of us have stood in a real ruin and felt exactly what those gardeners were selling.

What we love, I think, is not the past. It is the evidence that the past is over, and that we survived it.
